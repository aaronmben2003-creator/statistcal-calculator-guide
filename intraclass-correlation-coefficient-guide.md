# Intraclass Correlation Coefficient: A Guide to Statistical Reliability

## Introduction

Statistical analysis helps researchers understand data, measure consistency, and make informed decisions. One important statistical measure is the Intraclass Correlation Coefficient (ICC), which is commonly used to assess the reliability and agreement of measurements.

Researchers, students, and data analysts can use ICC to understand whether different raters or repeated measurements produce consistent results.

## What Is the Intraclass Correlation Coefficient?

The Intraclass Correlation Coefficient measures the degree of similarity between measurements. It is frequently used in healthcare research, psychology, education, and other fields that require reliable measurements.

ICC values are generally interpreted between 0 and 1, with higher values often indicating stronger reliability. However, interpretation depends on the ICC model and the research context.

## How Is ICC Used?

ICC can help researchers:

- Evaluate agreement between multiple raters.
- Measure the consistency of repeated observations.
- Assess the reliability of measurement instruments.
- Support research quality and reproducibility.
